# Cybersecurity Act 2025

Cybersecurity Act 2025 Section 1 to Act 14 of 2025 Page 5 C T CYBERSECURITY ACT 2025 Act 14 of 2025 AN ACT FOR THE ENHANCEMENT OF CYBERSECURITY IN TONGA I assent, TUPOU VI, 28 August 2025.

BE IT ENACTED by the King and Legislative Assembly of Tonga in the Legislature of the Kingdom as follows:

## PART I – PRELIMINARY

#### 1 Short title and Commencement

(1) ThisAct may be cited as the CybersecurityAct 2025.

(2) ThisAct shall come into force on a date proclaimed by Cabinet.

#### 2 Interpretation

In thisAct, unless the context otherwise requires –

“Board” means the Cybersecurity Advisory Board established in section 6 of this Act;

“CERT” means the Computer Emergency Response Team established in section 9 of this Act;

“critical infrastructure” means the physical, electronic or virtual infrastructure assets, systems and networks designated as such by the Minister CPR under section 11 of this Act, and for the purposes of this definition,

“virtual infrastructure assets” include any operating system-based or software-based infrastructure assets, whether locally or remotely accessed;

“cybersecurity incident” means any act or attempt, threat, successful or unsuccessful, to gain unauthorised access to, disrupt or misuse an information system or information stored on an information system;

“Minister CO” means the Minister responsible for cybersecurity operations;

“Minister CPR” means the Minister responsible for cybersecurity policy and regulation;

“Ministry CO” means the Ministry responsible for cybersecurity operations;

“Ministry CPR” means the Ministry responsible for cybersecurity policy and regulation; and

“operator of critical infrastructure” means the legal owner or operator of critical infrastructure, and in the case of joint ownership or joint operators, each such owner or operator.

#### 3 Act binds the Crown

ThisAct shall bind the Crown.

CYBERSECURITY

#### 4 Division of cybersecurity functions among ministries

(1) The Kingdom’s cybersecurity functions shall be divided as set forth in this Act among two Ministers –

(a) Minister CPR; and

(b) Minister CO.

(2) The Prime Minister shall designate the Ministers which shall serve as the Minister CPR and the Minister CO, respectively.

(3) Nothing in this Act shall prevent the Prime Minister from designating a single Minister to serve as both the Minister CPR and Minister CO.

## PART III – CYBERSECURITY POLICY AND REGULATION

### DIVISION 1 – POWERS, DUTIES AND FUNCTIONS

#### 5 Powers, duties and functions of the Minister CPR

(1) The Minister CPR shall have the following powers, duties and functions –

(a) make policies for the protection of critical infrastructure, other cybersecurity matters or any other matter relevant to the operation of thisAct;

(b) encourage the introduction of technological and administrative measures to enhance cybersecurity;

(c) issue and publishguidanceon cybersecuritymeasures to beimplemented by public and private sector entities or any other matter relevant to the operation of thisAct;

(d) order an operator of critical infrastructure take to remedial or protective or preventative action in connection with a reported cybersecurity incident, threat of such an incident, or the results of an assessment or audit;

(e) prepare legislative proposals, with a view to amend existing laws to strengthen cybersecurity in the Kingdom;

(f) establish one or more Computer Security Incident Response Teams, in addition to the CERT, to respond to cybersecurity incidents affecting the information systems of individual ministries, departments, and agencies of the Kingdom or any combination of the foregoing;

(g) facilitate the Kingdom’s compliance with internationally accepted cybersecurityprinciples, including any standards and obligations laid down by international agreements and treaties to which the Kingdom is a party;

(h) cooperate with organisations for the purpose of performing its functions, and assist such organisations to develop the policies, practices and procedures required for compliance with thisAct; and

(i) participate in international fora and engage and collaborate with supervisory authorities of other nations and regions, including through the exchange of information and coordination of policies and legislation relating to international cyberthreats and cybersecurity.

(2) In fulfilling its functions under thisAct, the Minister CPR shall have the power to do all other things authorised by thisAct or any relevant laws.

### DIVISION 2 – THE CYBERSECURITY ADVISORY BOARD

#### 6 Establishment of the Board

The Cybersecurity Advisory Board is hereby established.

#### 7 Members of the Board

(1) The Minister CPR shall serve as the Chair and a member of the Board.

(2) Subject to sub-section (3), the Minister CPR shall appoint members of the Board.

(3) The Minister CPR shall invite nominations for members of the Board from –

(a) the Attorney General;

(b) the Commissioner of Police;

(c) the National Reserve Bank of Tonga;

(d) such other operators of critical infrastructure as the Minister CPR may determine; and

(e) such other persons or entities as the Minister CPR may determine.

(4) An individual to be considered for appointment to the Board shall be –

(a) a person of integrity and good reputation; and

(b) qualified to serve as a member of the Board by virtue of that person’s knowledge of or experience in cybersecurity.

(5) The Minister CPR may decline to appoint any individual nominated and remove any sitting Board member who, in the Minister CPR’s sole determination, does not meet the requirements of subsection (4).

(6) Uponanyrefusalorremovalundersubsection (5),theMinister CPRshallinvite the relevant person or entity to nominate an alternative representative.

#### 8 Functions and duties of the Board

(1) The Board shall have the following functions and duties –

(a) serve as a forum for operators of critical infrastructure and law enforcement to share information on matters relating cybersecurity;

(b) advise the Minister CPR on matters relating to cybersecurity;

(c) provide feedback and suggestions on proposed or current policies, guidance or actions of the Ministry CPR or the CERT, as applicable; and

(d) perform any other functions and duties consistent with this Act as designated by the Minister CPR from time to time.

### DIVISION 2 – POWERS, DUTIES AND FUNCTIONS

#### 10 Powers, duties and functions of the Minister CO and CERT

(1) The Minister CO shall have the following duties and functions, which he shall delegate to the CERT –

(a) establish and coordinate national mechanisms to detect, share information about, mitigate and respond to cybersecurity threats;

(b) monitor cybersecurity incidents in the Kingdom, provide early warning, alerts, announcements and dissemination of information to relevant stakeholders about risks and incidents;

(c) receive reports of cybersecurity incidents from operators of critical infrastructure and share such reports with the Minister CPR;

(d) recommend to an operator of critical infrastructure that it take remedial, protective or preventative action in connection with a reported cybersecurity incident or threat of such an incident, and otherwise provide dynamic risk and incident analysis and situational awareness;

(e) propose to the Minister CPR that he issue an order that an operator of critical infrastructure take remedial action in connection with a reported cybersecurity incident;

(f) disseminate information relating to cybersecurity threats and incidents to operators of critical infrastructure, other stakeholders and the public as appropriate;

(g) assist law enforcement in analysing and investigating cybersecurity incidents;

(h) assist government ministries, departments and agencies on operational matters relating to cybersecurity;

(i) carry out programs to promote public awareness and education on matters relating to cybersecurity;

## PART V – CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE

### DIVISION 1 – DESIGNATION AND REGISTRATION

#### 11 Designation of critical infrastructure

(1) The Minister CPR shall designate any physical, electronic or virtual infrastructure assets, systems and networks as critical infrastructure if he considers such infrastructure assets, systems and networks to be essential for national security of the Kingdom or the economic or social well-being of its citizens.

(2) The Minister CPR shall publish in the Government Gazette any designations made under subsection (1).

(3) In determining whether to make a designation under subsection (1), the Minister CPR shall consider whether –

(a) the infrastructure assets, systems and networks are necessary for –

(i) the security, defence or international relations of the Kingdom;

(ii) the provision of services related to –

(A) electronic communications;

(B) banking or other financial services;

(C) electrical energy;

(D) public transportation; or

(E) other public utilities and public services;

(iii) the protection of public safety or public health, including systems related to essential emergency services; or

(iv) the functioning of the Government; or

(b) a disruption of the infrastructure assets, systems and networks would likely result in –

(i) the interruption of a life sustaining service, including the supply of water, health services and energy;

(ii) a material adverse effect on the economy of the Kingdom;

(iii) an event that would result in massive casualties or fatalities;

(iv) failure or substantial disruption of financial services in the Kingdom; or

(v) amaterialadverseeffectonthesecurityoftheKingdom,including intelligence and military services.

(4) Pursuant to sub-section (3), the Minister CPR shall remove a designation of critical infrastructure if the Minister CPR determines such designation is no longer warranted.

(5) Any removal of a designation under subsection (4) shall be published by the Minister CPR in the Government Gazette.

(6) The Minister CPR may take into account additional considerations when considering a designation under subsection (1) or removal of a designation under subsection (4).

#### 12 Registration of critical infrastructure

(1) The Minister CPR shall maintain an up-to-date register of designated critical infrastructure, including the identity and contact information of each operator of critical infrastructure and any other information required to be included by the Minister CPR.

(2) The Minister CPR shall make a copy of the register available to each registered operator of critical infrastructure.

(3) A registered operator of critical infrastructure shall notify the Minister CPR of any change in legal ownership of the critical infrastructure or any change in the person or entity responsible for operating such critical infrastructure.

(4) The Minister CPR shall issue and publish procedures for the registration and notification requirements under this section.

### DIVISION 2 – OBLIGATIONS OF OPERATORS OF CRITICAL

INFRASTRUCTURE

#### 14 Periodic reporting obligations

An operator of critical infrastructure shall conduct and submit copies of periodic cybersecurity assessments of such infrastructure to the Ministry CPR in a form and manner prescribed by the Minister CPR.

#### 15 Duty to report cybersecurity incident

(1) Within 24 hours of becoming aware thereof, an operator of critical infrastructure shall report to the CERT –

(a) a cybersecurity incident relating to the information systems relating to such critical infrastructure;

(b) a cybersecurity incident relating to any information system interconnected with or communicating with such information systems; or

(c) any other cybersecurity incident or other occurrence that the Minister CPR has designated as requiring such notification.

(2) The report required in subsection (1) shall be in a form prescribed by the Minister CPR.

#### 16 Obligation to take remedial action

(1) In connection with –

(a) an audit under section 13(2);

(b) an assessment under section 14; or

(c) a report under section 15, with or without any related request by the CERT to the Minister CPR to order remedial action, the Minister CPR may order an operator of critical infrastructure by to take remedial action.

(2) An operator of critical infrastructure shall comply with the order referred to in subsection (1).

#### 17 Obligation to maintain appropriate policies, practices and procedures

(1) An operator of critical infrastructure shall develop, maintain and implement such policies, practices and procedures as are necessary for it to –

(a) detect, share information about, mitigate and respond to cybersecurity threats and incidents; and Cybersecurity Act 2025 Section 18 to Act 14 of 2025 Page 13

(b) comply with its obligations under thisAct.

(2) An operator of critical infrastructure shall –

(a) communicate to its staff the policies, practices and procedures required by subsection (1);

(b) keep records evidencing its compliance with the requirements of this Division and any other applicable requirements relating to cybersecurity; and

(c) make available to the Minister CPR upon request copies of policies, practices, procedures, and records required by paragraph (b) and subsection (1)(a).

#### 18 Civil actions for recovery of pecuniary penalties

(1) The Attorney General may institute proceedings in a court of competent jurisdiction against an operator of critical infrastructure for contravention of any of the provisions of this Division.

(2) A proceeding under subsection (1) shall be commenced within 5 years after the relevant contravention concerned.

#### 19 Pecuniary penalties for contravention of this Act

(1) If a court of competent jurisdiction is satisfied that an operator of critical infrastructure has contravened a provision of this Division, the court may order that operator of critical infrastructure to pay such pecuniary penalty, in respect of each contravention, as the court determines to be appropriate.

(2) Subject to subsection (3), a pecuniary penalty under subsection (1) shall be an amount the court determines appropriate having regard to all relevant matters including –

(a) the nature and extent of the contravention;

(b) the nature and extent of any loss or damage suffered as a result of the contravention;

(c) the circumstances in which the contravention took place; and

(d) whether the operator of critical infrastructure has previously been found by acourtofcompetentjurisdiction inproceedings under thisAct tohave engaged in any similar conduct.

(3) Apecuniary penalty ordered under subsection (1) for a contravention –

(a) by an individual, shall not exceed $500,000; and

(b) by a body corporate, shall not exceed $1,000,000.
