# Inquests Act 2025

Inquests Act 2025 Section 1 to Act 7 of 2025 Page 5 C T INQUESTS ACT 2025 Act 7 of 2025 AN ACT TO ESTABLISH COMPREHENSIVE PROCEDURES AND STANDARDS FOR CONDUCTING INQUESTS IN CASES OF SUSPICIOUS DEATHS, ENSURING TRANSPARENCY, FAIRNESS AND ACCOUNTABILITY IN THE INVESTIGATION PROCESS, AND FOR OTHER RELATED PROCESSES I assent, TUPOU VI, 28 August 2025.

BE IT ENACTED by the King and Legislative Assembly of Tonga in the Legislature of the Kingdom as follows:

#### 1 Short Title

This Act may be cited as the Inquests Act 2025.

#### 2 Interpretation

In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires –

“cause of death” includes not only the apparent causeof death as ascertainable by inspection or post-mortem examination of the body of the deceased, but also all matters necessary to enable an opinion to be formed as to how the deceased came by his death and whether his death resulted in any way from, or was accelerated by, any negligent or unlawful act or omission on the part of any other person;

“CommissionerofPolice”meanstheCommissionerofPoliceappointedunder section 11 of the Tonga Police Act [Cap 7.07];

“Registrar General” means a person appointed under section 2 of the Births, Deaths and Marriages Registration Act [Cap 6.01]; and

“sudden unnatural death” means death where a person has -

(a) committed suicide;

(b) been killed by another, or by an animal or by machinery or during the course of a fire or by fatal accident;

(c) died under circumstances in which some other person may have committed an offence; or

(d) died and the cause of such death is not known.

#### 5 Post-mortem examination of body

(1) Subjecttosection4,amedicalpractitionershall,assoonaspracticable,perform a post-mortem examination of the body of the deceased.

(2) The medical practitioner, if it is necessary in order to ascertain the cause of death, shall extendtheexaminationtothedissectionof the bodyandan analysis of any portion thereof, and may cause any portion thereof to be transmitted to any investigating officer, analyst or other competent person.

(3) If a post-mortem examination is held in pursuance of the provisions of this section or pursuant to an order made under section 9(2) or if examination of a body is carried out in pursuance of this Act, the body or remains thereof shall not be buried or reburied, as the case may be, except under and in accordance with the order of a Magistrate, provided that a police officer of or above the rank of Chief Superintendent may order burial of the body.

(4) The provisions of subsection (3) shall not be interpreted as requiring an order of a Magistrate or of a police officer of or above the rank of Chief Superintendent to dispose of any portion of a body which may have been sent for examination under this Act to a medical practitioner, analyst or other competent person, and such portion may, unless a Magistrate otherwise orders, be disposed of in such manner as the Chief Executive Officer for Health may, by any general or special direction, direct.

#### 6 Report of medical practitioner, analyst or other competent person

(1) A medical practitioner, analyst or other competent person making a post- mortem examination under any of the provisions of this Act shall draw up a report in the form prescribed by Regulations.

(2) A medical practitioner shall as soon as practicable forward the original post- mortem cause of death certificate to the Registrar-General after making the post-mortem.

#### 7 Duty of Magistrate on receipt of report

(1) If, upon receiving all necessary reports, a Magistrate shall be satisfied, without holding an inquest, as to the cause of death, he shall report to the Attorney- General the cause of death as ascertained to his satisfaction.

(2) AMagistrateshallnotholdanyinquestunderthisActifhehasreasontobelieve that criminal proceedings against any person for having caused the death of the deceased have been, or are about to be, commenced.

(3) In all other cases, the Magistrate shall proceed as soon as possible to hold an inquest but may adjourn the inquest sine die if any such criminal-proceedings as aforesaid are commenced.

#### 8 Death of a person in custody

When any person dies while in the custody of the police, or prisons, a report shall be made as soon as practicable of such death to a Magistrate, and such Magistrate or another shall hold an inquest into the cause of death and, for such purpose, may request a police officer to make the investigation and report referred to in section 3.

#### 9 Powers of Magistrate

(1) A Magistrate holding an inquest shall have all the powers exercisable by a Magistrate in holding a criminal trial under the provisions of the Criminal Offences Act.

(2) A Magistrate holding an inquest, if he considers it expedient that the body of the deceased person should be examined by a medical practitioner in order to discover the cause of death, may, whether a post-mortem examination has been performed under the provisions of section 5 or not, issue an order to a medical practitioner to perform a post-mortem examination of such body, and may, for such purpose, order it to be exhumed.

#### 10 Magistrate may view body

A Magistrate holding an inquest may, if he so desires, view the body of the deceased and may, for that purpose, cause such body to be exhumed.

#### 11 Examination of witnesses

Any person who in the opinion of the Magistrate is a properly interested person shall be entitled to examine any witness at an inquest either in person or by a law practitioner, provided that the Magistrate shall disallow any question which in his opinion is not relevant or is vexatious, scandalous or oppressive.

#### 12 Order of examination

Unless the Magistrate otherwise determines, a witness at an inquest shall be examined first byhim andifthewitnessisrepresentedat theinquest,lastlybyhisrepresentative.

#### 13 Incriminatory questions

(1) No witness at an inquest shall be obliged to answer any question tending to incriminate himself.

(2) Whereitappearstothe Magistratethat awitnesshasbeenaskedsucha question under subsection (1), he shall inform the witness that he may refuse to answer.

#### 14 Persons whose conduct may be questioned

(1) Any person whose conductis likelyin the opinion of the Magistrateto be called in question at an inquest, shall, if not duly summoned to give evidence there at, begivenatleast 24hours’notice ofthedate,hourandplaceat whichtheinquest will be held.

(2) Iftheconduct ofanypersoniscalledinquestionat aninquest ongroundswhich the Magistrate thinks substantial and which relate to any matter referred to in section 15 and if that person is not present at the inquest and has not been duly summoned to attend or otherwise given notice of the holding of the inquest, the inquest shall be adjourned to enable him to be present, if he so desires.

#### 15 Matters to be ascertained at inquest

(1) The proceedings and evidence at an inquest shall be directed solely to ascertaining the following matters, and any matters relevant thereto, namely -

(a) who the deceased was;

(b) the cause and the date and place of death;

(c) the persons,if any, to be charged withmurder,manslaughter, infanticide, causing death by dangerous driving or of being accessories before the fact to such offences, should the Magistrate find that the deceased came by his death by murder, manslaughter, infanticide or by dangerous driving; and

(d) the particulars for the time being required under the provisions of the Births, Deaths and Marriages Registration Act [Cap 6.01] to be registered concerning the death.

(2) For the purpose of avoiding doubt it is hereby declared that a finding under subsection 1(c) shall not operate as a committal for trial nor shall such proceedings be deemed to be a preliminary inquiry.

#### 16 Opinion of Magistrate

The Magistrate shall not express any opinion on any matters other than those referred to in section 15, provided that nothing in this section shall preclude the Magistrate frommakingarecommendationdesignedtopreventtherecurrenceoffatalitiessimilar to that in respect of which the inquest is being held.

#### 17 Evidence

(1) Documentary evidence as to how the deceased came by his death shall not be admissible at an inquest unless the Magistrate is satisfied that there is good and sufficient reason why the author of the document should not attend the inquest.

(2) If such documentary evidence is admitted at an inquest, the inquest shall be adjourned to enable the author of the document to give oral evidence if the Magistrate desires.

(3) The Magistrate shall not be bound by any evidentiary law when holding an inquest but may attach such weight to any evidence as he shall think fit.

#### 18 Evidence and findings to be recorded

(1) Each Magistrate holding an inquest shall record the substance of the evidence and shall set out his findings in writing and shall as soon as practicable transmit to the Attorney-General and the Registrar of the Supreme Court, copies of his record of such evidence and findings, certified by him as correct.

(2) The original of such record and findings shall be retained by the registrar to the Magistrate’s court in which the inquest has been held.

#### 19 Place of Inquest

Theplaceinwhichanyinquestisheldshall bedeterminedbytheMagistrate, provided that a Magistrate conducting an inquest may exclude any person at any stage of the inquest.

#### 20 Powers of Attorney-General

(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of section 7, the Attorney-General may at any time apply to the Supreme Court for an order directing a Magistrate to hold an inquest into the cause of and the circumstances connected with, any death occurring in the circumstances specified in section 3 and the Magistrate to whom such direction is given shall thereupon proceed to hold an inquest in accordance with the provisions of the order and of this Act.

(2) When the proceedings at any inquest have been closed and it appears to the Attorney-General that further investigation is necessary, he may apply to the Supreme Court for an order directing the Magistrate or another to re-open the inquest and to make further investigation, and thereupon the Magistrate so directed, shall have full power to re-open the inquest and make further investigation,andthereaftertoproceedinthesamemannerasiftheproceedings at such inquest had not been closed:

Provided that this subsection shall not apply to any inquest at which a finding of murder, or culpable homicide not amounting to murder, has been returned against any person.

(3) When making an order under this section, the Supreme Court may also direct whether the body, if buried, shall or shall not be exhumed.

(4) If it is made to appear to the Supreme Court that it is expedient that an inquest commenced by one Magistrate should be continued by another, it may, by Inquests Act 2025 Section 21 to Act 7 of 2025 Page 11 order, direct both such Magistrates accordingly who shall thereupon comply therewith.

#### 21 Review and appeals

(1) Any interested parties shall have the right to seek judicial review of decisions made by the Magistrate during the course of an inquest.

(2) Any appeals arising from the findings and recommendations of an inquest shall be heard in accordance to applicable laws.

#### 22 Regulations

The Minister with the consent of Cabinet may make regulations for all or any of the following purposes -

(a) for regulating the practices and procedure at inquests in relation to matters not provided for in this Act;

(b) for regulating the forms to be used and all matters connected therewith;

(c) for regulating the records to be kept, and the returns to be made, by Magistrates exercising functions under this Act; or

(d) for the effective implementation and administration of the provisions and objects of this Act.

#### 23 Repeal

This Act repeals the Inquests Act [Cap. 3.09].
