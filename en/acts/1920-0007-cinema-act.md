# Cinema Act

AN ACT FOR THE REGULATION AND CONTROL OF CINEMATOGRAPH AND SIMILAR DISPLAYS1 Commencement [4th September, 1920]

#### 1 Short title

This Act may be cited as the Cinema Act.

#### 2 Power to make regulations

The Prime Minister with the consent of Cabinet may make regulations for —

(a) the control and censorship of cinema and other similar displays;

(b) the control and censorship of posters or advertisements relating to cinematograph and other similar displays;2

(c) the control and censorship of video tapes or films and other similar displays.3

#### 3 Supervision at exhibition to ensure safety from fire

It shall be lawful for the Minister of Police or any Inspector or Sergeant of Police to order any display to cease or to give any other directions which may be necessary for ensuring the safety from fire of the premises at which the display takes place or for ensuring the safety of the people attending the display.

#### 4 Power of entry

A member of the Police Force may at all reasonable times enter any premises in which he has reason to believe a cinema or other similar display is being given or is about to be given with a view to seeing whether the provisions of this Act and any regulations made thereunder have been complied with.4

#### 5 General penalty

Any person who obstructs the entry of any police officer in the execution of his duty under section 4 or who commits a breach of any of the provisions of this Act or of the regulations made thereunder shall be liable to a fine not exceeding $1,500.5

#### 6 Not applicable for private exhibitions

This Act shall not apply to a display given in private premises to which the public are not admitted.
