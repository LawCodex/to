#### 1 Short Title and commencement

(1) This Act shall be cited as the Tonga National University Act 2021.

(a) Part II (Establishment of the University); and

#### 4 Establishment of the University

(1) The Tonga National University is hereby established.

(2) At the commencement of this Act, the government owned institutions known as –

(a) Tonga Institute of Education;

(b) Tonga Institute for Higher Education;

(c) Tonga Institute of Science and Technology;

(d) Tonga Maritime Polytechnic Institute;

#### 5 Chancellor

(1) The King of Tonga shall be the Chancellor of the University.

(2) The Chancellor shall be the titular head of the University.

#### 6 Legal status

(1) The University is a body corporate with perpetual succession.

(2) The University shall be capable of –

(a) entering into contracts;

(b) acquiring, holding and disposing of property;

(c) suing and being sued; and

(d) doing all other things that a body corporate may lawfully do.

#### 7 Common seal

(1) The University shall have a common seal.

(2) The common seal shall be in a form approved by a written resolution of the Council.

(3) At all times, the common seal shall be kept in the custody of the secretary to the Council.

(4) The common seal shall be affixed to a document pursuant to a resolution of the Council to that effect.

#### 8 Objectives

(1) The objectives of the University are to –

(a) preserve, extend and disseminate knowledge in the Tonga through teaching, research, scholarship, consultancy or any other means;

(b) provide academic, technical and vocational training and continuing education that is responsive and appropriate to the needs of the people of Tonga;

(c) foster and facilitate –

(i) the study of the Tongan language and culture and all matters pertaining to this; and

(ii) research into subjects of relevance or significance to Tonga;

(d) facilitate the economic and social development of Tonga through educational pathways that foster innovation and sustainable use of resources; and

(e) achieve the vision, mission and goals of the University set out in its Annual Corporate Plan.

(2) The University shall –

(a) operate in accordance with and be based on Tongan values and principles;

(b) strive for excellence in teaching and research and the attainment of the highest standards in education;

(c) promote intellectual independence and autonomy as foundation principles of the University; and

(d) establish an educational system that is accessible, inclusive, relevant and sustainable for Tonga.

(3) The University shall offer academic, technical and vocational programmes and qualifications from certificate to post-doctorate level.

(4) Programmes of the University shall promote –

(a) knowledge of the Tongan language and culture through focussed research, teaching and publications;

(b) respect for the environment through initiatives that strengthen climate resilience and ensure sustainable use of resources; and

(c) dual use of the Tongan and English languages.

#### 10 Policy directives

## PART III – FACULTIES AND SCHOOLS

#### 11 Faculties and Schools

The Council in consultation with the Minister and with the consent of Cabinet shall establish the faculties and schools of the University.

#### 12 Establishment of additional faculties and schools

The Minister in consultation with the Council and with the consent of Cabinet may from time to time –

(a) establish a new faculty or school of the University under this Act; or

(b) close down, modify or rename a new or existing faculty or school of the University.

#### 13 Academic freedom

(1) The University shall ensure that the principles of academic freedom are preserved and advanced.

(2) The principles of academic freedom include –

(a) freedom of faculty members and students to –

(i) engage in research;

(ii) engage in intellectual debate without fear of censorship or retaliation;

Section 14 Tonga National University Act 2021 Page 14 Act 13 of 2021 to

(iii) within the law, question existing knowledge and theories and to put forward new ideas or to state controversial or unpopular opinions; or

(iv) express their views without fear of sanction, unless the manner of expression substantially impairs the rights of others or, in the case of faculty members, those views demonstrate that they are professionally ignorant, incompetent, or dishonest with regard to their discipline or fields of expertise;

(b) freedom of the University to appoint and retain its own staff;

(c) a guarantee that serious charges brought against a faculty member will be heard before a committee of his peers, providing the right to due process, including the assumption that the burden of proof lies with the person who brought the charges; and

(d) subject to this Act and the Annual Corporate Plan, the freedom of the University to –

(i) regulate its courses and subject matter of courses taught at the University; and

(ii) teach and assess students in a manner that it considers best promotes learning.

#### 14 Self accreditation

(1) The University shall be subject to the applicable law of the national accreditation authority.

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), the University may apply for self- accreditation in accordance with applicable requirements of the national accreditation authority.
