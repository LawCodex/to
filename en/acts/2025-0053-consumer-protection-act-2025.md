#### 3 Scope of the Act

(1) This Act shall bind the Crown, to the extent that the Crown engages in trade.

(2) Nothing in this Act renders the Crown liable to a pecuniary penalty or to be prosecuted for an offence.

#### 4 Objects of the Act

The objects of this Act are –

(a) to enhance the welfare of the people of Tonga through the promotion of fair trading and protection of consumers’ rights;

(b) to protect the long-term interests of the people of Tonga with regard to the availability, price, quality and reliability of goods and services; and

(c) to promote and encourage fair trading practices in markets in Tonga.

#### 5 Consumers' rights

(1) Any person, in their capacity as consumers, shall have the following rights –

(a) right to safety;

(b) right to choice;

(c) right to consumer education;

(d) right to information;

(e) right to representation; and

(f) right to redress.

(2) Recognition of the rights under subsection (1) does not confer or imply anyliabilityonthe Crownin respect of those rightsor their enforcement.

#### 24 Unsafe goods

(1) The Authority may publish on the Ministry’s website or any other form of communication, declare goods (or a class or classes of goods) to be unsafe goods if –

(a) there is no product safety standard in force for the goods; and

(b) the Authority determines that –

(i) the goods will or may cause injury to any person; or

(ii) a reasonably foreseeable use (including misuse) of the goods will or may cause injury to any person.

(2) In deciding whether to make a declaration under subsection (1)(b)(ii), the Authority shall have regard to all the circumstances, including the following –

(a) the likelihood of an injury occurring to a person as a result of the reasonably foreseeable use or misuse of the goods;

(b) the seriousness of the injury likely to be suffered by the user of the goods or any other person;

(c) whether such injury is likely to recur;

(d) any steps that the supplier or manufacturer of the goods has taken to mitigate the risk of injury; and

(e) whether, taking into account the ordinary and intended use of the goods, the public interest favours making a declaration.

## PART IV – GUARANTEES IN RESPECT OF GOODS

#### 30 Guarantees provided under the Act

A consumer has the following guarantees in respect of goods supplied in trade –

(a) the trader or manufacturer can give good title to the goods;

(b) goods when delivered shall arrive on time and in an acceptable condition;

(c) goods shall be of an acceptable quality;

(d) goods shall be fit for a particular purpose;

Section 31 Consumer Protection Act 2025 Page 26 Act 39 of 2025 to

(e) goods shall match the description given to the consumer;

(f) goods shall be a reasonable price (when the price is not set); and

(g) goods shall have spare parts and repair facilities available.

#### 31 Goods covered by this Part

(1) Guarantees under this Part shall apply to all goods except those referred to in subsection (3).

(2) Guarantees under this Part shall apply despite any provision to the contrary in any agreement.

(3) Guarantees under this Part shall not apply to –

(a) commercial goods the value of which is higher than the value prescribed in Regulations;

(b) goods supplied otherwise than in trade;

(c) goods bought at auction or by competitive tender; or

(d) goods supplied by a charitable organisation for the principal purpose of benefitting the person to whom the supply is made.

#### 32 Guarantees as to title

Where goods are supplied in trade to a consumer the following guarantees apply –

(a) that the supplier has a right to sell the goods;

(b) that the goods are free from any undisclosed security; and

(c) that the consumer has the right to undisturbed possession of the goods, except in so far as that right is varied pursuant to —

(i) a term of the agreement for supply, where that agreement is a hire purchase agreement; or

(ii) a security, or a term of the agreement for supply, in respect of which the consumer has received —

(A) verbal advice, acknowledged in writing by the consumer, as to the way in which the consumer’s right to undisturbed possession of the goods could be affected, sufficient to enable a reasonable consumer to understand the general nature and effect of the variation; and

(B) a written copy of the part thereof which provides for the variation.

Consumer Protection Act 2025 Section 33 to Act 39 of 2025 Page 27

#### 33 Guarantees as to delivery

Where a supplier is responsible for delivering, or for arranging for the delivery of, goods to a consumer, in trade, there is a guarantee that the goods shall be received by the consumer —

(a) at a time, or within a period, agreed between the supplier and the consumer; or

(b) if no time or period has been agreed, within a reasonable time.

#### 34 Guarantee as to acceptable quality

(1) Where goods are supplied in trade to a consumer there is a guarantee that the goods are of acceptable quality.

(2) For the purposes of subsection (1), goods are of acceptable quality if, according to the reasonable consumer standard, they are —

(a) fit for all the purposes for which goods of the type in question are commonly supplied;

(b) acceptable in appearance and finish;

(c) free from major and minor defects;

(d) safe; and

(e) durable.

(3) In subsection (2), the reasonable consumer standard means the standard that would be applied by a reasonable consumer fully acquainted with the state and condition of the goods, including any hidden defects, having regard to —

(a) the nature of the goods;

(b) the price (where relevant);

(c) any statements made about the goods on any packaging or label on the goods;

(d) the nature of the supplier and the context in which the supplier supplies the goods;

(b) the document is physically delivered to the person to whom the document is intended to be served, or sent directly by email; or

(c) the document is delivered to the notified legal representative of the person to whom the document is intended to be served.

#### 145 Annual report

The Minister shall present the Annual Report of the Ministry to Cabinet, which shall cover the activities of the Division and the Authority under this Act for the preceding year.

#### 146 Confidentiality

(1) Except as provided by subsection (2), a person engaged in the administration of this Act shall not disclose to any person any information or evidence given, or the contents of any document produced, without the written permission of the Minister given in relation to the disclosure.

(2) The Minister shall only grant the permissionreferredtoin subsection (1) after –

(a) the trader or manufacturer has been given an opportunity to make any representations; and

(b) the Minister is satisfied (after due consideration) that the disclosure would be in the public interest.

(3) It is not a contravention of subsection (1) if, in any legal proceedings a person disclosesanyinformation, evidenceor contentsreferred tointhat subsection in answering a question that a person is compelled to answer in those proceedings.

#### 147 Regulations

(1) The Minister may, with the consent of Cabinet, make Regulations for giving effect to and carrying out the purposes of this Act.

(2) Regulations may provide for any or all of the following —

(a) fees, forms, and any other matter related to this Act;

(b) the manner in which complaints may be made and investigations conducted;

(c) information that a trader or manufacturer or a service provider is required to provide to a consumer in relation to charges for services;

(d) information that a trader or manufacturer is required to provide to the Ministry prior to or after the importation or supply of goods;

(e) prescribed standards; and

(f) any other thing required by this Act to be prescribed.

#### 148 Repeal and savings

(1) The Consumer Protection Act [Cap. 17.04] is repealed.

(2) Notwithstanding subsection (1), the Consumer Protection (Product Safety & Labelling Standards) Regulations continue in force (with such modifications as are necessary) as if made under this Act.
