# His Majesty’s Armed Forces Regulations

HIS MAJESTY'S ARMED FORCES REGULATIONS1 Made under section 141 of His Majesty's Armed Forces Act Commencement [4th May, 1994]2

#### 1 Short title

These Regulations may be cited as the His Majesty’s Armed Forces Regulations.3

#### 2 Definitions

(1) In these Regulations “the Act” means the His Majesty’s Armed Forces His Majesty’s Armed Forces Act. 4

(2) Unless the contrary intention appears, expressions in these Regulations have the same meaning as if these Regulations formed part of the Act.

OFFICERS

#### 3 Qualifications for grant of commission

A person shall not be appointed or issued with a commission (other than an honorary commission) as an officer of the Force unless:

(a) he is a citizen of the Kingdom of Tonga;

(b) he is medically fit; and

(c) he has the educational qualifications laid down by the Board.

#### 4 Rank on first appointment

(1) Subject to this regulation a person appointed to be an officer of the Force shall be appointed with a rank not higher than second lieutenant.

(2) A person appointed to be an officer of the Force who possesses professional qualifications entitling him to be licensed to practice as a medical practitioner, dental practitioner or law practitioner, or who possesses other professional or technical qualifications approved by the Board, may be appointed with the rank of lieutenant or a higher rank.

(3) A person may be appointed and granted an honorary commission in the Force in a rank and subject to the conditions which may be approved by His Majesty.

(4) A person appointed and granted an honorary commission shall not by reason only of that appointment be entitled to receive the pay, allowances or other emoluments of an officer of the Force.5 REGULAR FORCE

#### 5 Promotion 6

(1) Subject to this regulation an officer of the Regular Force is eligible to be promoted to the higher substantive rank specified on completion of the following periods of commissioned service in the lower substantive rank specified:

(a) to lieutenant after 2 years as second lieutenant;

(b) to captain after 6 years as lieutenant;

(c) to major after 7 years as captain.

(2) An officer of the Regular Force shall not be promoted to a specified rank unless his promotion is recommended by the Chief of the Defence Staff.

(3) Where under the provisions of sub-regulation (1) an officer is entitled to be promoted to a specified rank but the Chief of the Defence Staff decides that he is unable to recommend the promotion of the officer, the Chief of the Defence Staff shall make a report to His Majesty setting out the circumstances and the reasons for the decision.

(4) Where the Chief of the Defence Staff has made a report to His Majesty under sub-regulation (3), a copy of the report shall be shown to the officer concerned who shall initial the report.

(5) An offer of the Regular Force may be promoted to a specified rank notwithstanding that provision is made for the appointment which he holds in the Force to be held by an officer of lower rank.

#### 6 Seniority

(1) Subject to this regulation, officers of the same rank in the Force shall take seniority in accordance with the respective dates of their appointments or promotions to that rant.

(2) Where an officer possessing professional qualifications is promoted or appointed to the Force, His Majesty, taking account of-

(a) the experience of the officer, and

(b) the period of time normally required to gain those professional qualifications, may fix the seniority of that officer in a rank at a date earlier than his appointment to that rank.

(3) Where an officer immediately before he was appointed and commissioned as an officer was serving in the Regular Force as a warrant officer, non- commissioned officer or soldier, His Majesty, on the recommendation of the Board and taking account of the service of the officer as a warrant officer and in the lower ranks, may fix the seniority of that officer in a rank at a date earlier than his appointment to that rank.

(4) For the purposes of sub-regulation (3) account is to be taken of previous service in the Regular Force as follows:

(a) one half of the period of service as a warrant officer; and

(b) one third of the period of service in the lower ranks, provided that the first ten years service in the lower ranks be disregarded.

(5) In computing periods of service for the purpose of this regulation, a period of service in the Territorial Force or Reserve shall be disregarded. 7

#### 7 Temporary or acting rank

(1) Subject to this regulation where an officer is posted to an appointment the rank for which is higher than the rank held by the officer, he may be granted that rank as a temporary or acting rank.

(2) An officer holding temporary or acting rank under sub-regulation (1) shall relinquish such rank on ceasing to hold the appointment for which that rank was granted.

(3) An officer holding temporary rank under sub-regulation (1) shall continue to hold such rank during absence from duty not caused by his own default unless the period of absence exceeds 3 months.

(4) An officer holding temporary rank under sub-regulation (1) shall cease to hold that rank on the substantive appointment of an officer to the appointment in respect of which the temporary rank was granted.

(5) An officer who has been granted acting rank under sub-regulation (1) shall hold that rank only during the period when he is performing the duties of the appointment for which the acting rank was granted.

(6) Officers holding temporary or acting rank shall take precedence amongst themselves according to their substantive rank and shall be junior to all officers holding corresponding substantive rank.

#### 8 Local rank

(1) Local rank may be conferred by the Chief of the Defence Staff on an officer for a period that is necessary in the interests of the service. 8

(2) An officer holding local rank-

(a) shall not be entitled to the pay, allowances or other emoluments for that rank; and

(b) shall only be liable to deductions for rations and quarter or for mess fees at the rate applicable to his substantive rank.

#### 9 Period of first appointment

(1) Where the first appointment of a person as a commissioned officer of the Force is for a specified period, such appointment shall be for a period of 6 years.

(2) At the termination of the period of appointment His Majesty may extend the period of the appointment for a further period or for further periods of 3 years.

#### 10 Age for transfer to the Reserve

(1) Subject to this regulation officers of the Regular Force shall be transferred to the Reserve on reaching the age of 47 years.

(2) Where the Chief of the Defence Staff certifies that it is necessary in the interests of the service, an officer may be retained in the Regular Force for a period not exceeding 5 years after the officer has reached the age of 47 years and at the end of the period of retention the officer shall be transferred to the Reserve. 9

(3) Subject to sub-regulation (7) of this regulation, where an officer is transferred to the Reserve under sub-regulation (1) the officer shall remain in the Reserve for 5 years.

(4) An officer who has not been promoted to the rank of captain after 10 years commissioned service may be transferred to the Reserve.

(5) His Majesty may approve the transfer to the Reserve of an officer who has not reached the age referred to in sub-regulation (1).

(6) Where the transfer of an officer to the Reserve has been approved under sub- regulation (5) the transfer may be for a specified period or for an unspecified period.

(7) No officer shall remain in the Reserve after he has reached the age of 52 years.

#### 11 Age for compulsory retirement10

(1) Subject to this regulation the age for the compulsory retirement of an officer of the Regular Force is-

(a) in the case of officers:

(i) colonel and above, 60 years;

(ii) lieutenant colonel, 56 years;

(iii) major, 56 years;

(iv) captain, 50 years;

(v) lieutenant, 50 years;

(vi) second lieutenant, 40 years,

(b) in the case of other ranks:

(i) warrant officer class 1, 60 years;

(ii) warrant officer class 2, 55 years;

(iii) staff sergeant, 50 years;

(iv) sergeant, 45 years;

(v) corporal, 40 years;

(vi) lance corporal, 35 years;

(vii) private, 30 years.

(2) Notwithstanding sub-regulation (1), the Board may determine that it is desirable in the interests of the Force that an officer of the Regular Force be retained in the Force after the officer has reached the age for compulsory retirement, but such retention shall not allow an officer to be engaged beyond 60 years.

Provided, that where the officer to be retained is of the rank of colonel or above, the compulsory age for retirement shall be the age to be determined by His Majesty upon consultation with the Chief of the Defence Staff, but shall not be beyond 65 years of age.11

#### 12 Resignation

(1) Subject to this regulation, upon giving 6 months notice in writing under his hand addressed to the Board, an officer of the Force may-

(a) tender his resignation as a commissioned officer of the Force; or

(b) apply to retire from the Force.

(2) An application and tender of resignation under sub-regulation (1) shall be delivered to the Chief of the Defence Staff who shall forward the application or resignation to the Board together with a certificate stating whether-

(a) all service and regimental claims have been paid;

(b) he is aware of any outstanding public claims;

(c) there is any objection to the resignation or retirement being approved; and

(d) there is any reason why the officer should not serve in the Reserve.

(3) The Board may, reject the resignation or application to retire of an officer if it is tendered in time of way, defence emergency or insurrection.

(4) Where the resignation or application of an officer to retire is accepted by His Majesty, the resignation or retirement shall take effect, unless otherwise ordered by His Majesty.

(5) Where an officer whose resignation has been rejected so requests, His Majesty may approve the transfer of the officer to the Reserve.

#### 13 Posting of officers

(1) Subject to this regulation, the Chief of the Defence Staff may, in time of defence emergency and with the approval of His Majesty-

(a) post an officer of the Regular Force to a position in the Reserve; or

(b) post an officer of the Reserve to a position in the Regular Force.

(2) Unless he has been called out for continuous full time defence service under the provisions of the Act, an officer of the Reserve shall not be posted to the Regular Force under sub-regulation (1) without the consent of the officer.

(3) For the purposes of regulation 5 service in the Regular Force by an officer who has been posted to that Force under this regulation shall be treated as service in the Reserve. 12

#### 14 Retirement on medical grounds

(1) Subject to this regulation, an officer who is certified by a medical officer to be unfit for military service on account of ill health or injury shall be retired.

(2) In this regulation “medical officer” means a qualified medical practitioner appointed by the Board to examine members of the Force.

(3) If an officer who has been certified unfit for military service on account of ill health or injury so request, His Majesty may approve the transfer of the officer to the Reserve.

#### 15 Requirement to continue serving after completing course of training

outside the Kingdom

(1) An officer who is selected to attend a course of instruction outside the Kingdom shall be required to enter into an undertaking that he will not tender his resignation or apply to be transferred to the Reserve for a specified period after he has completed the course.

(2) For the purposes of sub-regulation (1) the specified period is a period equal to the duration of the course of instruction plus one year.

(3) For the purpose of calculating the period specified period by sub-regulation

(2) a period of duration of a course of instruction that is less than a year shall be counted as a year.

TERRITORIAL FORCE

#### 16 Promotion

(1) Subject to this regulation the substantive promotion of officers in the Territorial Force shall be made by His Majesty on the recommendation of the Board.

(2) An officer of the Territorial Force shall not be promoted to the next higher rank unless his promotion has been recommended by the Chief of the Defence Staff to the Board.13

#### 17 Retirement

(1) Subject to this regulation, the age for the compulsory retirement of an officer of the Territorial force is 47 years.

(2) His Majesty may permit an officer of the Territorial Force who has reached the age of retirement referred to in sub-regulation (1) to continue in the service for such further period as he may specify.

#### 18 Transfer to the Reserve

(1) An officer of the Territorial Force may apply to be transferred to the Reserve.

(2) When an officer of the Territorial Force is transferred to the Reserve he shall remain in the Reserve for 5 years.

(3) Subject to regulation 17(2) no officer of the Territorial Force shall remain in the Reserve after he has reached the age of 52 years.

#### 19 Resignation and retirement on medical grounds

The provisions of regulations 12 and 14 apply to officers of the Territorial Force as if they were officers of the Regular Force.

THE RESERVE

#### 20 Age for the retirement

(1) Subject to this regulation and regulations 10(7) and 18(3) the age for the compulsory retirement of an officer of the Reserve is 52 years.

(2) His Majesty may permit an officer of the Reserve who has reached the age of retirement referred to in sub-regulation (1) to continue in the service for such further period as he may specify.

MEMBERS OTHER THAN OFFICERS ENLISTMENT

#### 21 Attestation

(1) The officer recruiting or enlisting a person in the Force shall:

(a) satisfy himself that the person understands the contents of the attestation paper set out in the second schedule to the Act including the general conditions of enlistment;

(b) warn the person to be enlisted that if he knowingly makes a false answer to any question in the attestation paper which is put to him, he will be liable to be punished as provided by the Act;

(c) satisfy himself that the person offering to enlist is over the apparent age of eighteen years, or, if under that age, that the person is over the age of 16 years and has obtained the consent required by section 25(2) of the Act;

(d) read or cause to be read to the person the questions set out in the attestation paper and ensure that a written answer has been made to each question.

(2) After he has complied with the provisions of sub-regulation (1), the officer recruiting or enlisting a person into the Force shall-

(a) ask the person to make and sign the declaration set out in the attestation paper as to the truth of the answer given to the questions on that paper; and

(b) administer to him the oath of allegiance or solemn affirmation set out in the attestation paper.

(3) Upon signing the declaration in the attestation paper and upon taking the oath or making the solemn affirmation the person shall become a soldier of the Force subject to military law as provided by section 139 of the Act.

(4) The officer recruiting or enlisting a person into the Force shall sign the attestation paper and such signature shall confirm that the requirements of the Act and of this regulation have been duly compiled with.

#### 22 Approval of enlistment

(1) The duly completed attestation paper duly dated shall be delivered to the Chief of the Defence Staff or to an officer authorised by the Chief of the Defence Staff to receive it.

(2) The Chief of the Defence Staff or other officer receiving the attestation paper shall sign it and date it immediately below the signature of the recruiting officer to signify that the enlistment of the person in the Force is approved.14

#### 23 Rank on enlistment

(1) Subject to this regulation a person enlisted in the Regular Force shall, on enlistment, be given the rank of private.

(2) A person enlisted in the Regular Force who-

(a) possesses technical qualifications approved by the Board; or

(b) has previously served in the Force as a warrant officer or non- commissioned officer, may, with the approval of the Board, be given on enlistment a rank higher than the rank of private.

#### 24 Promotion

Promotion in the Regular Force shall be by selection based on merit.

#### 25 Requirement to re-engage for further service after completion of

course of training outside the Kingdom

(1) A warrant officer, non-commissioned officer or a soldier who is selected to attend a course of instruction outside the Kingdom shall be required to re- engage in advance in the Regular Force for a specified period.

(2) For the purposes of sub-regulation (1) the period for which a warrant officer, non-commissioned officer or a soldier shall be required to re-engage in advance is the duration of the course of instruction plus one year.

(3) For the purposes of this regulation, the duration of a course of instruction or part of a course of instruction that is less than a year shall be counted as a year.

#### 26 Age for transfer to the Reserve

(1) Subject to this regulation the age for transfer to the Reserve of members of the Regular Force other than officers is 47 years.

(2) Where the Chief of the Defence Staff certifies that it is in the interests of the Force that a soldier be retained in the Regular Force beyond the age specified in sub-regulation (1) the age for the transfer to the Reserve of that soldier is 50 years. 15

(3) No soldier shall remain in the Reserve after he reaches the age of 52 years.

(4) Where the Board certifies that it is desirable in the interest of the Force that a soldier of the Regular Force be retained in the Force after the soldier has reached the age that, but for this sub-regulation would be the age for the compulsory retirement of the soldier, the age for the compulsory retirement of that soldier is 60 years.16 DISCHARGE

#### 27 Certificate of discharge

A certificate of discharge in relation to a soldier of the Force shall contain the following particulars-

(a) number;

(b) name, including Christian or forenames;

(c) date and place of enlistment;

(d) physical description of soldier on leaving the Regular Force;

(e) rank of soldier on leaving the Regular Force;

(f) assessment of conduct and character on leaving the Regular Force signed by the officer making the assessment;

(g) date of discharge;

(h) reason for discharge;

(i) total period of service in the Regular Force on discharge; and

(j) signature of issuing officer.

#### 28 Reasons for discharge

For the purposes of section 33 of the Act, the other grounds on which a soldier of the Regular Force may be discharged are-

(a) that the soldier is guilty of any misconduct as a soldier under Part VI of the Act;

(b) that the soldier is guilty of misconduct other than as a soldier (including conviction by a civil court on a charge which in the opinion of the Chief of the Defence Staff renders it undesirable for him to continue serving as a soldier);

(c) that the soldier is medically unfit for service;

(d) compassionate reasons;

(e) that the soldier is inefficient in the performance of his duties;

(f) that the services of the soldier are no longer required;

(g) that the soldier has reached the age prescribed by These Regulations for his discharge;

(h) that he has claimed his discharge under section 34.

#### 29 Issue of warrant to warrant officers

A warrant in the form set out in the Second Schedule to these Regulations and signed by the Chief of the Defence Staff shall be issued to every person appointed to the substantive rank of warrant officer in the Regular Force. 17

#### 30 Reduction of warrant officers for inefficiency or unsuitability

(1) Subject to this regulation where a warrant officer of the Regular Force is considered by his commanding officer to be inefficient in the performance of the ordinary duties of his rank, or unsuitable as a warrant officer, the warrant officer may be reduced in rank by the Chief of the Defence Staff. 18

(2) Reduction in rank of a warrant officer under sub-regulation (1) shall not take effect until it has been confirmed by the Board.

#### 31 Reduction of non-commissioned officers for inefficiency or

unsuitability

(1) Where a non-commissioned officer of the Regular Force is considered by his commanding officer to be inefficient in the performance of the ordinary duties of his rank, or to be unsuitable as a non-commissioned officer, the non- commissioned officer may be reduced in rank-

(a) in the case of a staff sergeant or sergeant, by the Chief of the Defence Staff; and

(b) in the case of a corporal or lance corporal by his commanding officer.

(2) Where a soldier of the Regular Force holding acting rank is considered by his commanding officer to be inefficient in the performance of the soldier may be ordered by his commanding officer to relinquish that rank and revert to a lower acting rank or to his substantive rank.

THE TERRITORIAL FORCE

#### 32 Notice paper

The attestation paper to be given to a person wishing to enlist in the Territorial Force shall be in the form set out in the Second Schedule to the Act with the notifications set out in the First Schedule to these Regulations.

#### 33 Attestation

The method of attestation shall be the same as that prescribed for persons offering to enlist in the Regular Force.

#### 34 Rank on enlistment

(1) Subject to this regulation a person enlisting in the Territorial Force shall be given on enlistment the rank of private.

(2) A person enlisted in the Territorial Force who-

(a) possesses technical qualifications approved by the Board; or

(b) has previously served in the Force as a warrant officer or non- commissioned officer, may, with the approval of the Board, be given on enlistment a rank higher that the rank of private.

#### 35 Promotion

Promotion in the Territorial Force shall be by selection based on merit.

#### 36 Issue of form of warrant to warrant officers

A warrant in the form set out in the Second Schedule to these Regulations and signed by the Chief of the Defence Staff shall be issued to every person appointed to the substantive rank of warrant officer in the Territorial Force.19

#### 37 Reduction in rank for unsuitability or inefficiency

(1) Subject to this regulation where a warrant officer or non-commissioned officer of the Territorial Force is considered by his commanding officer to be inefficient in the performance of the ordinary duties of his rank, or unsuitable as a warrant officer or non-commissioned officer, the warrant officer or non- commissioned officer may be reduced in rank:

(a) in the case of a warrant officer, staff sergeant or sergeant by the Chief of the Defence Staff; and

(b) in the case of a corporal or lance-corporal by his commanding officer.

(2) The reduction in rank of a warrant officer of the Territorial Force shall not take effect until it has been confirmed by the Board.

(3) Where a soldier of the Territorial Force holding acting rank is considered by his commanding officer to be inefficient in the performance of the ordinary duties of his rank, or unsuitable to hold the acting rank, the soldier may be ordered by his commanding officer to relinquish that rank and revert to a lower acting rank or to his substantive rank. 20

#### 38 Reasons of discharge

For the purposes of section 111 of the Act, the grounds upon which a soldier of the Territorial Force may be discharged are-

(a) that the soldier is guilty of misconduct as a soldier under Part VI of the Act;

(b) that the soldier is guilty of misconduct other than as a soldier (including conviction by a civil court on a charge which in the opinion of the Chief of the Defence Staff renders it undesirable for him to continue serving as a soldier).

(c) that the soldier has failed to complete the training prescribed for the purposes of section 103 of the Act;

(d) that the soldier is medically unfit for service;

(e) compassionate reasons;

(f) that the soldier is inefficient in the performance of his duties;

(g) that the services of the soldier are no longer required;

(h) that the soldier has reached the age prescribed by these regulations for his discharge;

(i) that he is entitled to be discharged under section 111(2).

#### 39 Training

(1) For the purposes of section 103 of the Act the training that a member of the Territorial Force is required to undergo is-

(a) during the first year of his service 6 weeks preliminary training;

(b) during each subsequent year annual training in camp for a period of 14 days.

(2) In addition to the periods of training set out in sub-regulation (1) a member of the Territorial Force may attend such further periods of voluntary training as are approved by the Chief of the Defence Staff.

(3) The Chief of the Defence Staff may exempt a member of the Territorial Force from carrying out all or part of the training referred to in sub-paragraph (1) (a) or (1) (b).

#### 40 Notice of time and place of training

(1) The place and time appointed for the training of a member of the Territorial Force may be notified by means of a public notice exhibited at the headquarters of the unit to which the person concerned belongs.

(2) The notification referred to in sub-regulation (1) shall be sufficient notice of the time and the place appointed for such training and individual notification need not be made to the member.

#### 41 Place of attendance on call out

For the purposes of section 106 of the Act, the place where a member of the Territorial Force shall attend when called out on temporary service or on continuous full time defence service shall be the headquarters of the unit to which he belongs.

Provided that the unit headquarters in the outer islands shall be deemed to be

“headquarters” for the purposes of this regulation.

#### 42 Age for transfer to the Reserve

(1) Subject to this regulation the age for the transfer of soldiers of the Territorial force to the Reserve is- private soldiers - 30 years lance corporals and corporals - 35 years warrant officers and non-commissioned - 40 years officers other than lance corporals or corporals

(2) Where the Chief of the Defence Staff certifies that it is in the interests of the Force, a soldier may be retained beyond that age. 21 THE RESERVE

#### 43 Period of service

(1) Subject to this regulation, the term of service for which, in accordance with section 115(2) of the Act a member may be re-engaged in the Reserve is 6 years.

(2) A member of the Reserve shall not be re-engaged for a period which would extend past the date on which he reaches the age of 52 years.

#### 44 Grounds for Discharge

For the purposes of section 122 of the Act the grounds upon which a soldier of the Reserve may be discharged are-

(a) that the soldier is guilty of misconduct as a soldier under Part VI of the Act;

(b) that the soldier is guilty of any misconduct other than as a soldier (including conviction by a civil court on a charge which in the opinion of the Chief of the Defence Staff renders it undesirable for him to continue serving in the Reserve);

(c) that the soldier is medically unfit for service;

(d) compassionate reasons;

(e) that the soldier has failed to report in accordance with regulation 46;

(f) that the services of the soldier are no longer required; and

(g) that the soldier has reached the age prescribed by the Regulations for his discharge.

#### 45 Place of attendance on call out

For the purposes of section 117 of the Act the place where a member of the Reserve shall attend when called out on continuous full time defence service shall be the headquarters of the His Majesty’s Armed Forces .

Provided that the unit headquarters in the outer islands shall be deemed to be

“headquarters” for the purpose of this regulation.

#### 46 Notification of change of address

(1) A member of the Reserve shall report any change of his address to the Chief of the Defence Staff as soon as practicable but in any case within 21 days of the change.

(2) In addition to any report under sub-regulation (1), a member of the Reserve shall report in writing, giving his permanent address to the headquarters of the His Majesty’s Armed Forces once every year on the anniversary of his entry into the Reserve.

ORGANISATION

#### 47 Composition of the Force

(1) The Force consist of three forces-

(a) The Regular Force;

(b) The Territorial Force; and

(c) The Reserve.

(2) Each force of the Force is composed of units of-

(a) The Tonga Royal Guards;

(b) The Royal Tonga Marines;

(c) The Navy; and

(d) such other units as His Majesty may from time to time designate under Section 13 of the Act.

#### 48 Establishment

(1)

(a) The numerical establishment of the Regular Force shall not be more than 1,500 officers made up as follows-

(i) Force Headquarters;

(ii) Land force;

(aa) Royal Guards;

(bb) Royal Marines;

(iii) Royal Navy;

(iv) Support Units;

(aa) Logistics Unit;

(bb) Rural Development Unit;

(cc) Military Police;

(dd) Royal Corp of Musicians;

(v) Air Wing; and

(vi) Force Training.

(b) The Chief of the Defence Staff may determine the ratio of officers to other ranks in the Force and the numerical establishment of the Force units and sub-units of the Force.22 DRESS AND EQUIPMENT

#### 49 Care of equipment

(1) Every member of the Force is responsible for the safe keeping and proper care of arms, ammunition, uniform and equipment issued to him.

(2) A member of the Territorial Force or of the Reserve shall only-

(a) wear an item of uniform issued to him;

(b) use arms or ammunition issued to him; or

(c) use any equipment issued to him, when he is on duty.

#### 50 Uniforms

(1) Members of the Force shall wear such uniform as is prescribed.

(2) The prescribed uniform shall be issued to members of the Force at public expense.

(3) An item of uniform which has become unserviceable by reason of-

(a) fair wear and tear;

(b) loss or damage attributable to service; or

(c) loss or damage attributable to another cause outside the control of the member, shall be repaired or replaced at public expense.

#### 51 Orders of dress and scales of equipment

(1) The orders of dress and scales of equipment of the Force and units of the Force shall be set out in Armed Forces Orderss.

(2) The badges and insignia of the Force and units of the services shall be set out and described in Armed Forces Orderss.

(3) The colours of the Force shall be red and white.

PAY AND ALLOWANCES

#### 52 Rates of pay to be determined by Board

The rates of pay applicable to-

(a) members of the Regular Force;

(b) members of the Territorial Force attending for training or called out on temporary service or on continuous full time defence service under sections 103, 104 or 105 respectively of the Act; and

(c) members of the Reserve when called out on continuous full time defence service under section 116 of the Act, shall be the rates determined from time to time by the Board under section 144 of the Act.

#### 53 Allowances to be determined by Board

The allowances applicable to-

(a) members of the Regular Force;

(b) members of the Territorial Force attending for training or called out on temporary service or continuous full time defence service under section 103, 104 or 105 respectively of the Act; and

(c) members of the Reserve called out on continuous full time defence service under section 116 of the Act, shall be the rates determined from time to time by Board under section 144 of the Act.

#### 54 Pay and allowances during leave

Where leave, other than leave without pay, is granted to-

(a) a member of the Regular Force;

(b) a member of the Territorial Force who has been called out on temporary service or on continuous full time defence service; or

(c) a member of the Reserve who has been called out on continuous full time defence service, the member shall be entitled to his normal rate of pay and allowances during the period of leave.

#### 55 Pay when holding acting rank

A member of the Force holding acting rank shall not be entitled to the pay and allowances for that rank, but shall continue to receive pay and allowances at the rate appropriate to his substantive or temporary rank.

#### 56 Pay when holding temporary rank

A member of the Force holding temporary rank shall be entitled to the pay and allowances for that temporary rank.

#### 57 Period when entitled to pay

A member of the Force shall receive pay and allowances at the rate appropriate to his rank from and including the day on which he assumes duty in that rank until he ceases to be a member of the Regular Force or assumes another rank.

#### 58 Pay on reversion to lower rank

Where a member of the Force reverts from a temporary rank to a lower substantive rank he shall not entitlement to any increment of pay earned in that temporary rank, and if subsequently promoted substantively to the rank previously held temporarily or re-appointed to a higher temporary rank he shall be entitled only to the minimum rate of pay for that rank.

#### 59 Pay liable to deductions

(1) Subject to this regulation and the provisions of Part VII of the Act the pay of a member of the Force shall be liable to deduction by order of the Board or the Chief of the Defence Staff to meet any public or service claim that there may be against him.

(2) The pay of a member of the Force shall be available to meet allfines, deductions, stoppages and forfeitures to which he is properly liable except that he shall be entitled to receive pay at not less than the rate referred to in section 98(3) of the Act unless it has been forfeited under section 99 of the Act.

#### 60 Allowances not liable to deductions

An allowance payable to a member of the Force under the provisions of these Regulations shall not be liable to deduction.

#### 61 Forfeiture of pay

The pay to which a member of the Force is entitled under these Regulations shall not be forfeited except as provided in Part VII of the Act.

#### 62 Calculation of pay while prisoner of war

(1) Where under the provisions of section 99(2) of the Act the pay of a member of the Force is forfeited for the period during which he was a prisoner of war, pay shall be forfeited for every day of absence from the date of capture or from the first day of the month in which the conduct of the member which led to the forfeiture occurred whichever is the earlier.

(2) Where a member of the Force who has been absent as a prisoner of war rejoins for duty, pay for the period from the first day of the month in which he was released to the day before that on which he joins for duty may be withheld pending an investigation by the board for the purposes of section 99(2) of the Act.

#### 63 Investigation of barrack damage

(1) Without prejudice to proceedings under another section of the Act the cause and extent of every damage or loss to which section 100 of the Act applies and the time at which damage or loss was occasioned shall be investigated by a court of inquiry convened-

(a) if more than one unit or part of a unit is concerned, by the Chief of the Defence Staff, or

(b) if only one unit or part of a unit is concerned, by the commanding officer of that unit.

(2) The amount which a person may be required under section 100 of the act to contribute towards compensation for any damage or loss shall be the amount of the damage or loss divided by the number of persons who under that section are required to contribute towards compensation for the said damage or loss.

(3) Where any part of the amount of the damage or loss has been written off, or is the subject of an application to be written off as a charge against the public revenue under the regulations for the time being in force relating to write-off, the amount of damage or loss for the purpose of section 100 of the Act shall be the total amount of the damage or loss less the part which has been written off, or is the subject of an application to be written off.

(4) For the purpose of sub-regulation (2), the calculation of the number of persons by which the amount of the damage or loss is to be divided shall not include persons who it appears from an investigation made under sub-regulation (1) could not have occasioned or assisted in occasioning the damage or loss.

LEAVE AND TRAVEL

#### 64 Annual leave

(1) Unless the requirements of the service make it impracticable to do so a commanding officer shall arrange for leave of absence, known as per annum leave, to be granted annually to members of the Regular Force.

(2) Per annum leave may be granted to a member of the Regular Force at the rate of 36 days leave in respect of each complete year of service.

(3) In respect of periods of service of less than a year, per annum leave accrues at the rate of 3 days per calendar month.

#### 65 Annual leave not accumulative

(1) Subject to this regulation per annum leave shall not be accumulated, and leave not taken prior to the thirty-first day of March in the year next following the year in which it was earned, shall be forfeited.

(2) On the application of the member concerned, the Chief of the Defence Staff may extend the date referred to in sub-regulation (2) to the thirty-first day of May of that year.

(3) Where prior to the date these Regulations come into effect a member of the Regular force has accumulated per annum leave in excess of 36 days the Chief of the Defence Staff may allow the member to carry the accumulated leave forward.

(4) Where a member wished to accumulate his entitlement to per annum leave for-

(a) the purpose of taking leave outside the Kingdom; or

(b) another purpose approved by the Chief of the Defence Staff, the Chief of the Defence Staff may allow the member to accumulate per annum leave up to 108 days.

#### 66 Recording applications for leave

(1) Application for leave of absence by a member of the Regular Force shall be made on the form prescribed by Armed Forces Orders.

(2) A record of leave of absence granted to a member of the Regular Force shall be kept in the book prescribed by Armed Forces Orders.

#### 67 Embarkation leave

A member of the Regular Force posted for duty outside the Kingdom shall be entitled to three days leave prior to embarkation.

#### 68 Entitlement to travel at public expense on annual leave

(1) Subject to this regulation a member who is granted per annum leave shall be entitled to the cost of a return passage by sea at public expense to his home in the Kingdom.

(2) The class travel to which members travelling under this regulation are entitled shall be- for officers: first class (Minimum available); for warrant officers and non-commissioned officers of the rank of sergeant and above: second class (Minimum available); for corporals and below: deck passenger.

(3) Where second class accommodation is not available members entitled to that class of travel shall travel as deck passengers.

#### 69 Additional leave for travel to home island group

In addition to per annum leave to which he may be entitled, a member who is granted free travel under the provisions of regulation 69 shall be entitled to leave for the purpose of travelling for the period of the voyage to and from the first port of call of the island group in which his home is located, not exceeding the following periods for the single journey-

(a) from Tongatapu District:

(i) to the Vava’u District: 2 days

(ii) to the Ha’apai District: one day;

(iii) to Niuatoputapu or Niuafo’ou: 4 days;

(iv) to any other part of the Kingdom: one day;

(b) from Ha’apai District:

(i) to the Tongatapu District: one day;

(ii) to the Vava’u District: 2 days;

(iii) to Niuatoputapu or Niuafo’ou: 3 days;

(iv) to any other part of the Kingdom: one day.

(c) fromt he Vava’u District:

(i) to the Tongatapu District: 2 days;

(ii) to the Ha’apai District: one day;

(iii) to Niuatoputapu or Niuafo’ou: 2 days;

(iv) to any other part of the Kingdom: one day;

(d) from Niuatoputapu or Niuafo’ou:

(i) to the Tongatapu District: 4 days;

(ii) to the Vava’u District: 2 days;

(iii) to the Ha’apai District: 3 days;

(iv) to any other part of the Kingdom: 2 days.

#### 70 Sick leave, Regular Force

(1) Subject to this regulation a member of the Regular Force who is ill or injured may be granted sick leave on full pay for a period not exceeding 28 days in a calendar year with the approval of the Chief of the Defence Staff.

(2) The Chief of the Defence Staff may approve the granting of sick leave to a member for any period or periods not exceeding a total of 28 days in a calendar year.

(3) Subject to the approval of the Chief of the Defence Staff where the Director of Health recommends, the period of sick leave granted to a member may be extended up to 42 days on full pay in a calendar year and for a further period up to a total of 90 days on half pay in a calendar year.

(4) When sick leave in excess of 28 days in a calendar year is approved by the Chief of the Defence Staff, he shall submit a report to the next session of the Board stating the reason, cause and the nature of the illness or injury.

(5) Illness or injury necessitating a member being granted sick leave should only be approved on production of a certificate issued by a medical officer or a medical practitioner.

(6) Where a member is on an island where it is impossible to obtain a medical certificate sick leave may be granted if the Chief of the Defence Staff is satisfied that the member’s illness or injury is genuine.

(7) In all cases where the illness or injury of a member is attributable to his own negligence or misconduct, the Chief of the Defence Staff may with the approval of the Board withhold the granting of sick leave.

#### 71 Short leave

(1) The Chief of the Defence Staff may grant leave as he thinks fit to enable a member to attend to urgent affairs of a private nature.

(2) The leave granted under sub-regulation (1) shall be deducted from the member’s entitlement to per annum leave.

#### 72 Leave on public holidays

In addition to per annum leave granted under regulation 66 the Chief of the Defence Staff may grant leave for public holidays and the Christmas vacation is may be declared for the Tonga Civil Service.

#### 73 Leave subject to exigencies of the service

The granting of leave, other than sick leave, shall be subject to the exigencies of the service permitting.

#### 74 Paid leave and leave without pay

(1) Subject to this regulation and regulation 73, leave granted to a member under this Part shall be leave on full pay.

(2) The Chief of the Defence Staff may grant leave of absence without pay to a member of the Regular Force for a period not exceeding two years.

#### 75 Sick leave, Territorial Force

(1) Subject to this regulation a member of the Territorial Force who comes ill or is injured in the course of duty may be granted sick leave or a period not exceeding 28 days in a calendar year.

(2) Subject to the approval of the Chief of the Defence Staff, where the Director of Health recommends, the sick leave granted to a member of the Territorial Force may be extended up to 42 days in a calendar year.

(3) A member of the Territorial Force who becomes ill or who is injured otherwise than in the course of duty may be granted sick leave not exceeding days in a calendar year.

(4) Unless with the approval of the Board, sick leave granted to a member of the Territorial Force for any one period shall not extend beyond date on which the pay of that ember would normally have ceased had not become injured or ill.

(5) The Chief of the Defence Staff may approve the granting of sick leave to a member of the Territorial Force for any period of periods not exceeding a total of days in a calendar year.

(6) When sick leave in excess of 28 days in a calendar year is approved the Chief of the Defence Staff, he shall submit a report to the next session of the Board among the reason, cause and the nature of the illness or injury.

(7) Illness or injury necessitating a member of the Territorial Force being granted to sick leave should only be approved on production of a leave certificate and by a medical officer or medical practitioner.

(8) Where the illness or injury of a member of the Territorial Force attributable to his own negligence or misconduct the Chief of the Defence Staff may hold the granting of sick leave.

MEDICAL TREATMENT

#### 76 Member of Regular Force

A member of the Regular Force shall be entitled to receive medical (including hospital), dental and optical treatment at public expense.

#### 77 Member of Territorial Force during training

(1) Subject to this regulation a member of the Territorial Force who becomes ill or who is injured during a period of training shall be entitled to receive treatment for the illness or injury at public expense.

(2) The period of entitlement to treatment at public expense referred to in sub- regulation (1) shall not extend beyond the period of training which the member was obliged to undergo at the time of his illness or injury without the approval of the Board.

#### 78 Member of Territorial Force or the Reserve on continuous full time

defence service

(1) Subject to this regulation a member of the Territorial Force or the Reserve who is rendering continuous full time defence service shall be entitled to receive medical (including hospital), dental and optical treatment at public expense.

(2) The period of entitlement to treatment at public expense referred to in sub- regulation (1) shall not extend beyond the period the member is required to render continuous full time defence service, without the approval of the Board.

FEMALE PERSONNEL

#### 78A Female Personnel23

(1) Female members of the Force may marry and continue to serve and shall maintain the same professionalism, discipline and physical fitness as their male counterparts.

(2) Female members of the Force may be considered for His Majesty’s Commission upon the successful completion of relevant officers training and recommendation of the Chief of the Defence Staff.

(3) Notwithstanding this regulation, no female members of the Force shall serve on His Majesty’s ships.

(4) There shall be no sexual relationship between members of the Force.

MISCELLANEOUS

#### 79 Ranks in the Force

(1) The ranks of officers of the Force in order of seniority, commencing with the most senior rank, are-

(a) General Officers/Flag Officers:

Brigadier General, equivalent or higher;

(b) Field Officers:

Colonel or its equivalent;

Lieutenant-Colonel or its equivalent; and Second Lieutenant or its equivalent;

(c) Tactical Officers:

Captain or its equivalent;

Lieutenant of its equivalent; and Second Lieutenant or its equivalent.24

(2) Officers posted to or performing duty with naval units may wear the corresponding naval rank in accordance with the following table- lieutenant-colonel - commander major - lieutenant-commander captain - lieutenant lieutenant - sub-lieutenant second lieutenant - ensign

#### 80 Power of command and discipline

(1) Subject to this regulation an officer appointed to command a unit shall exercise command and have powers of discipline over all members serving in that unit, irrespective of seniority.

(2) An officer appointed as second in command of a unit shall exercise command over all members serving in that unit, irrespective of seniority, except the officer appointed to command the unit.

(3) In the absence of the commanding officer, the officer appointed as second in command of a unit shall have the same powers of discipline over all members of that unit as the commanding officer.

(4) In the absence of both the commanding officer and the second in command of a unit, the senior officer of the unit shall exercise command over all members of the unit, but shall not have powers of discipline unless specifically conferred by the commanding officer.

(5) A person in command of an aircraft, whether or not he is a member of the Force, shall in relation to all matters concerning the control or sfety of the aircraft, have power of command over all members of the Force who are on board the aircraft in their capacity as members of the Force.

#### 81 Precedence of officers

(1) Officers of the Regular Force shall take precedence over officers of the Territorial Force or of the Reserve of the same rank.

(2) Officers holding substantive rank shall take precedence over officers holding the same temporary or acting rank, and shall take precedence among themselves according to their seniority.

(3) Officers holding temporary or acting rank shall take precedence over officers holding the same local rank, and shall take precedence among themselves according to the respective dates of their appointment to the temporary or acting rank.

(4) Officers holding the same local rank shall take precedence among themselves according to the respective dates of their appointment to the local rank.

#### 82 Precedence of soldiers

(1) The ranks of soldiers in the Force, in order of precedence, are- senior warrant officer; warrant officer class 1; warrant officer class 2; staff sergeant; sergeant; corporal; lance corporal; and private.

(2) Warrant officers and non-commissioned officers of the Regular Force shall take precedence over warrant officers and non-commissioned officers of the Territorial Force or of the Reserve of the same rank.

(3) Soldiers holding substantive rank shall be senior to soldiers holding the same acting rank.

(4) The seniority of soldiers holding the same substantive rank shall be determined according to the dates of their respective promotion to that rank.

(5) Soldiers holding acting rank shall be senior to soldiers holding the same local rank.

(6) The seniority of soldiers holding the same acting rank shall be determined according to the respective dates of their promotion to that acting rank.

(7) The seniority of soldiers holding the same local rank shall be determined according to the respective dates on which local rank was granted to them.

(8) Where the respective dates referred to in sub-regulation (6) or sub-regulation

(7) in relation to any two soldiers are the same the seniority of the soldiers shall be determined according to the respective dates of promotion to the next lower rank at which seniority can be clearly established, including seniority as a private soldier.

#### 83 Precedence in the Regular Force

Personal form other forces who are seconded to the Regular Force shall take precedence among members of the Regular Force of equivalent rank according to the respective dates of their appointment to that rank.

#### 84 Form to be issued when punishment of imprisonment imposed

Where a commanding officer dealing with a charge under section 79 of the Act imposes a punishment of imprisonment upon a person the commanding officer shall-

(a) make an order in the form set out in Part 1 of the Third Schedule to these Regulations; and

(b) cause the order to be delivered to the civil prison along with the person named in the order.

#### 85 Form to be issued under sections 128 and 129 of Act

Where under the provisions of section 128 or 129 of the Act a Magistrate’s Court orders that a person who is illegally absent is to be delivered into military custody the certificate which is required by section 131(1) of the Act to be handed over to the appropriate military authority at the time of delivery of the person into custody shall be in the form set out in Part II of the Third Schedule to these Regulations.

#### 86 Form to be issued under section 130 of Act

Where under the provisions of section 130 of the Act a person is delivered into military custody without being brought before a Magistrate’s Court the certificate which is required by section 131(2) of the Act to be handed over to the appropriate military authority at the time of delivery shall be in the form specified in Part III of the Schedule to these Regulations.

#### 87 Form to be issued under section 137 of Act

(1) Where under the provisions of section 137 of the Act a person in military custody is committed into civil custody in Tonga for temporary detention the order required by that section shall-

(a) be in the form set out in Part IVA of the Third Schedule to these Regulations; and

(b) be signed by the commanding officer of the person committed.

(2) Where the commanding officer of the person in custody orders his release it shall be in the form set out in Part IVB of the Third Schedule to these Regulations.

#### 88 Service books for purposes of section 136 of Act

(1) The service books and other documents in which records may be made for the purposes of section 136(d) of the Act shall be- Unit order books Force Routine and Pay and Record Orders Register of Deserters Guards reports Conduct reports Conduct sheets Record of service Records of reports of courts of inquiry illegal absence of officers and soldiers.

(2) The record of a report of a court of inquiry on illegal absence of officers and soldiers referred to in sub-regulation (1) shall be in the form specified in Part V of the Third Schedule of these Regulations.

#### 89 Appropriation of fines imposed under Part VI of Act

All fines imposed under Part VI of the Act shall, when recovered, be paid to the Treasury to be placed to the credit of an account to be known as the “Defence Force Fund” to be applied for such purposes connected with the Force and in such manner as the Board shall from time to time approve.
