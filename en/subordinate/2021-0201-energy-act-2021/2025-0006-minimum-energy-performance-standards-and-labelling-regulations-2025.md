## PART II – MEPSL REGULATIONS

#### 3 Establishment of the MEPSL Regulations

The MEPSL Requirements are hereby established.

#### 4 Duties of trader or manufacturer regarding MEPSL

(1) A trader or manufacturer shall not make available for sale, lease, hire or purchase an item of a product class as described in Schedule 1 to any person in the Kingdom unless –

(a) the trader or manufacturer has registered the model in Australia or in New Zealand or other approved jurisdiction;

(b) a label that complies with the relevant Australia or New Zealand or other approved jurisdiction requirements for that item’s product class is displayed in front of that item;

(c) the model and brand designations on the label corresponds to the model and brand designations of that item; and

(d) the energy performance characteristics on the label corresponds to those registered in Australia or New Zealand or other approved jurisdiction for that model.

(2) Labels from countries or regions other than Australia or New Zealand or other approved jurisdiction that are related to energy efficiency, energy consumption or the cost of energy to operate the item, may be attached to the item instead of the Australia or New Zealand or other approved jurisdiction energy performance label, if the model is sold in limited numbers in the Kingdom. The definition of what constitutes limited numbers for models within a particular product class will be define in the MEPSL Code of Practice.

#### 5 Requirement to provide information for statistical purposes

If requested by the Energy Department in writing, a person who imports into the Kingdom an energy using item subject to MEPSL regulations shall provide the following information no later than 30 working days after receiving the request –

(a) the number of items in each product class specified by the Energy Department that the person sold to consumers in the Kingdom in each of the preceding 2 calendar years; and

(b) the energy performance characteristics of the item as specified in the request.

#### 6 Confidentiality of information

(1) This regulation applies to any information that a trader or manufacturer provides to the CEO under regulation 5.

(2) Subject to sub-regulation (3), the Director shall ensure that there is no unauthorized access to information provided by staff or members of the public and that information is released only for the purposes of a prosecution under these Regulations.

(3) Notwithstanding sub-regulation (2), information may be released or published if it is non-specific and the identity of the trader or manufacturer to which the information relates cannot be readily determined or identified.

## PART III – INSPECTIONS

#### 7 Power to inspect

(1) For the purposes of giving effect to the provisions in the Act, an enforcement officer shall have the power to inspect the premises of a trader or manufacturer during the normal business hours of that importer, trader or manufacturer.

(2) An inspection may be carried out as part of an enforcement taskforce, or may be carried out by two or more officers at a time.

(3) Subject to sub-regulation (1), the designation of enforcement officers shall be made in accordance to section 136 of the Act with its prescribed form.

#### 8 Inspection protocol

(1) Subject to the Act, an inspection shall be carried out according to the following inspection protocol – Minimum Energy Performance Standards and Labelling Regulations 2025 Regulation 9 to Page 9

(a) the lead enforcement officer will request to speak to the owner or person in charge of the premises and will inform such person of the purpose for the visit;

(b) the lead enforcement officer will introduce himself and the accompanying officers and show their identification cards; and

(c) the lead enforcement officer will direct and oversee the inspection of premises.

(2) If the trader or manufacturer refuses approval for entry, an enforcement officer may call on the assistance of a police officer.

(3) Following an inspection of the premises, where the lead enforcement officer determines that there has been a breach of these Regulations, the following process shall apply –

(a) the enforcement officer shall clearly identify the provision under the Act or these Regulations which has been breached in a Notification Form in Schedule 4 Form 3;

(b) the enforcement officer shall list the names of goods to be removed from shelves or other action required on the Notification Form and then sign the form;

(c) the trader or manufacturer shall checkand sign a copyof the Notification Form; and

(d) the original copy of the Notification Form shall be kept by the enforcement officer, and a duplicate form given to the owner.

(4) The Notification Form shall clearly state the time within which the owner is required to remedy the breach, following which there will be a re-inspection of the premises.

(5) Where the lead enforcement officer determines that –

(a) it would be in the public interest that certain goods should be removed from the shelves immediately; or

(b) there has been a directive that specific goods should be recalled or not be made available for sale to the public; the enforcement officer may, in accordance with regulation 9 order the immediate removal of the goods from the premises.

#### 9 Removal of goods

## PART IV – INVESTIGATIONS

#### 10 Power to investigate

(1) If an enforcement officer, based on reasonable grounds, suspects that a trader or manufacturer has committed an offence under these Regulations, an enforcement officer shall request a police officer to accompany him to the premises for the purposes of carrying out an investigation into the alleged offence.

(2) The power of an enforcement officer to inspect and search a premise, property or possession of a person under sub-regulation (1) shall be in accordance with sections 137 and 138 of the Act.

#### 11 Investigation protocol

An investigation shall be carried out according to the following inspection protocol –

(a) the lead enforcement officer shall ask for the owner or person in charge of the premises and, if applicable, show a copy of the search warrant;

(b) the lead enforcement officer shall oversee the search of the premises and obtaining the statements of those people present;

(c) if applicable, the enforcement officers shall seize goods, take photographs, obtain copies or take samples in relation to the search; and

(d) items seized shall be categorised, recorded, prepared and stored as exhibits by the police officers in the search team according to internal protocol of Tonga Police.

## PART VIII – OFFENCES AND PENALTIES

#### 20 Impersonating an enforcement officer

A person who impersonates or falsely represents himself to be an enforcement officer under the Act is guilty of an offence and is liable to a fine not exceeding $50,000, or a term of imprisonment of up to 6 months, or both.

#### 21 Forgery

A person who forges, falsifies, duplicates or produces a false document is guilty of an offence and is liable to a fine not exceeding $50,000, or a term of imprisonment of up to 6 months, or both.

#### 22 Destruction of evidence

A person who discards, destroys, hides, removes or alters any document, docket, register, tag, record or thing with the intent to avoid the detection of an offence under these Regulations, is guilty of an offence and is liable –

(a) in the case of an individual, to a fine up to $50,000, or a term of imprisonment of up to 6 months, or both; or

(b) in the case of a business, to a fine of up to $100,000, or a term of imprisonment of up to 12 months, or both.

#### 23 Unauthorised removal of goods in secure storage

A person who, without authorisation discards, destroys, hides, removes, alters or tampers withanygoodthatwas takenintosecure storage under regulation 10,isguilty of an offence and is liable to a fine not exceeding $50,000, or a term of imprisonment of up to 6 months, or both.

#### 24 Failure to maintain or provide records

A person who does not keep, maintain or provide a record as required under these Regulations is guilty of an offence and is liable –

(a) in the case of an individual, to a fine up to $50,000, or a term of imprisonment of up to 6 months, or both; or

(b) in the case of a business, to a fine of up to $100,000, or a term of imprisonment of up to 12 months, or both.
