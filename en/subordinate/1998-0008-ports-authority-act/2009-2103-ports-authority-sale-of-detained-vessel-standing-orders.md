# Ports Authority (Sale of Detained Vessel) Standing Orders

Ports Authority (Sale of Detained Vessels) Standing Orders CAP 22.07.04 to 2020 Revised Edition Page 3 C T PORTS AUTHORITY (SALE OF DETAINED VESSELS) STANDING ORDERS Made under sections 47 and 79(y) of the Ports Authority Act Commencement [18 August, 2009]1

1. These Standing Orders may be cited as the Ports Authority (Sale of Detained Vessel) Standing Orders.

2. The Authority's statutory lien on any vessel pursuant to section 47 of the Ports Authority Act 1998 is for the amount of all fees or rates payable to it and remaining unpaid.

3. Where the Authority has detained any vessel pursuant to section 47 of the Ports Authority Act 1998, the Authority may sell the vessel in order to redeem the unpaid fees or rates.

4. The Authority shall not sell any detained vessel without the leave of the Supreme Court and the Supreme Court shall not give leave except on proof

(a) that a sum is due to the Authority in relation to the vessel;

(b) that default has been made in the payment thereof.

5. For the purpose of bringing the proposed application to the notice of persons whose interest may be affected by the determination of the Supreme Court and of affording to any person an opportunity of becoming a party to the proceedings on the application, the Authority shall, at least 14 days prior to applying for leave to sell a vessel under these Standing Orders - CAP 22.07.04 Ports Authority (Sale of Detained Vessels) Standing Orders Page 4 2020 Revised Edition to

(a) publish such a notice in any local newspaper as provided for in Order 7, and

(b) serve, unless in that case it is impracticable to do so, such a notice as aforesaid, in the manner provided in Order 8 on each of the following persons:

(i) the person in whose name the vessel is registered;

(ii) any person who appears to the Authority to be the owner or agent for the vessel;

(iii) any person who appears to the Authority to be the charterer of the vessel whether or not by demise;

(iv) any person who appears to the Authority to be the operator of the vessel;

(v) any person who appears to the Authority to be a mortgagee of the vessel;

(vi) any other person who appears to the Authority to have a proprietary interest in the vessel.

6. If any person has been served with a notice under Order 5 informs the Authority within 7 days of the service of the notice of his desire to become a party to the proceedings the Authority shall make that person a defendant to the application.

7. A notice under Order 5 shall -

(a) state the nationality and registration marks of the vessel;

(b) state the type or details of the vessel;

(c) invite the person to whom the notice is given to inform the Authority in writing within 7 days of the service of the notice if he wishes to become a party to the proceedings on the application.

8. A notice under Order 5 shall be served by delivering it to the person to whom it is to be sent, or by leaving it at his usual or last place of business or abode.

9. If leave is given by the Court to the sale of the vessel the Authority shall ensure that the vessel is sold for the best price that can reasonably be obtained.

10. Failure to comply with any requirement of Order 5 in respect of any sale, shall not, after a sale has taken place, be a ground for impugning its validity.

11. The proceeds of any sale under these Standing Orders shall be applied -

(a) first, in payment of any duty or tax owing to the Government;

(b) secondly, in payment of the expenses of the sale;

(c) thirdly, in payment of the fees and rates and other incidental costs, due to the Authority;

(d) fourthly, in payment of other claims or liens of which written notice has been given to the Authority;

(e) fifthly, by paying upon demand any surplus to the person entitled to it, or if no such claim is made within one year of the date of the sale, the Authority shall be entitled to retain such surplus and all other rights to it are extinguished.

12. If a vessel fails to attract a buyer during the sale process under these Standing Orders, the Authority may, with leave of the Supreme Court, otherwise dispose of the vessel.
